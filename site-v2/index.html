01 — The bet

Getting something moving is easy. Getting it used is hard.

Plenty of student teams can get a mechanism moving. Fewer end up with something people reach for every day. That gap is where we work: we start where the job happens, watch how it is really done, and only then decide what to build.

The physical build leads — frame, circuits, firmware — and software and design serve it. The result fits the people around it, so it lands faster on the floor with less rework.

  1. Go to the site. Walk the floor, talk to the people doing the work, gather photo reference.
  2. Name the real pain. Turn what we saw into requirements before sketching a single solution.
  3. Build, then revise. Fabricate, wire, program, and check each version against the site — not the brief.

Second story

Bicycle-parking ecosystem — 1st runner-up (2nd place), S$2,400

Faced with illegally parked and discarded bicycles at a hackathon, the move was to leave the desk: we walked the neighbourhood, interviewed people, and gathered photo reference before designing anything.

  • The real pain was not laziness — there was nowhere good to park and no signal showing free spots.
  • So the answer was an ecosystem, not a gadget: a smart rack that makes parking right the easy option and records parking duration; computer vision on the racks to flag long-parked or abandoned bikes; a mobile app to find racks and free spots.
